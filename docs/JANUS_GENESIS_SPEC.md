# JANUS™

## Joint Adversarial Neural/User Screening

### Technical Architecture, Research Model, Threat Model, Telemetry and Verification Protocol

**Project:** JANUS™ by Onefold\
**Document status:** Foundational technical specification / research
blueprint\
**Version:** 0.1 --- Genesis\
**Core thesis:** Traditional CAPTCHAs ask a human to demonstrate a
capability machines lack. JANUS inverts the test: it searches for
challenge spaces in which artificial agents exhibit statistically
distinguishable response distributions, then accumulates evidence about
the *origin of cognition* rather than scoring correctness.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 0. Executive thesis

CAPTCHA historically depends on an asymmetry:

> a task is easy for humans and hard for machines.

That asymmetry has repeatedly expired. OCR, object recognition, visual
reasoning and increasingly general multimodal reasoning have all moved
from machine weaknesses to machine strengths.

JANUS starts from a different premise:

> Intelligence is not required to be weaker than human intelligence to
> be distinguishable from human cognition.

A model can solve a task better than a person and still solve it
**differently**. It may prefer different options, assign different
salience, converge too consistently on latent regularities, respond
differently to ambiguity, exhibit different conditional entropy, or
follow characteristic trajectories across a sequence of decisions.

JANUS therefore does not primarily ask:

> "Did the subject solve the challenge?"

It asks:

> "Which generative process is more likely to have produced this
> sequence of decisions?"

The protocol treats verification as sequential statistical inference
between at least two hypotheses:

-   **H:** interaction generated by a natural human participant.
-   **A:** interaction generated or materially selected by an artificial
    agent.

For observations (O\_{1:n}), the verifier seeks:

\[ P(H `\mid `{=tex}O\_{1:n}) `\quad `{=tex}`\text{and}`{=tex}
`\quad `{=tex}P(A `\mid `{=tex}O\_{1:n}) \]

rather than a binary correct/incorrect score.

The long-term research objective is to discover, measure and
continuously refresh **Human--AI Cognitive Divergence Surfaces
(HACDS)**: regions of challenge space where conditional response
distributions remain measurably different.

JANUS is not intended to prove metaphysical humanity. It estimates
whether the observable interaction is more compatible with calibrated
human behavior or calibrated artificial-agent behavior under a defined
threat model.

# 1. Why JANUS exists

## 1.1 The expiration problem of classical CAPTCHA

Classical CAPTCHA can be described as searching for a task (T) such
that:

\[ P(`\text{success}`{=tex}`\mid `{=tex}H,T)
`\gg `{=tex}P(`\text{success}`{=tex}`\mid `{=tex}A,T) \]

The system fails when machine capability catches up:

\[ P(`\text{success}`{=tex}`\mid `{=tex}H,T)
`\approx `{=tex}P(`\text{success}`{=tex}`\mid `{=tex}A,T) \]

Every static capability test therefore carries a technological
half-life.

JANUS instead searches for:

\[ D(P_H(R`\mid `{=tex}C),P_A(R`\mid `{=tex}C)) \> `\delta`{=tex} \]

where:

-   \(C\) is a challenge/context,
-   \(R\) is the response,
-   (P_H) is the human response distribution,
-   (P_A) is an artificial-agent response distribution,
-   \(D\) is a divergence measure,
-   (`\delta`{=tex}) is the minimum useful separation.

The challenge need not be difficult. It may deliberately have **no
objectively correct answer**.

## 1.2 The inversion

Traditional CAPTCHA:

1.  Present difficult task.
2.  Define correct answer.
3.  Human hopefully succeeds.
4.  Machine hopefully fails.

JANUS:

1.  Select a challenge with known human/model divergence.
2.  Permit a broad response space.
3.  Observe the response and interaction.
4.  Update evidence about its generating process.
5.  Select the next challenge conditionally.
6.  Stop once sufficient confidence or uncertainty is reached.

This inversion changes the arms race. An attacker no longer merely needs
a better solver. A successful adversary must produce behavior whose
**joint conditional distribution** resembles that of humans under
challenges whose discriminative feature may be unknown to the adversary.

# 2. The active principle: conditional randomness and model pseudo-randomness

## 2.1 "Random" model output is not equivalent to unconstrained randomness

A generative model commonly samples from a conditional distribution:

\[
x\_{t+1}`\sim `{=tex}P\_`\theta`{=tex}(x\_{t+1}`\mid `{=tex}x\_{`\leq `{=tex}t},C)
\]

Sampling can introduce stochasticity, but the distribution itself is
structured by model parameters, context, decoding policy and system
constraints.

Therefore two statements can simultaneously be true:

-   individual model outputs may be difficult to predict;
-   populations of model outputs may exhibit stable statistical
    preferences.

JANUS is interested in the second property.

## 2.2 Conditional pseudo-randomness as a fingerprint surface

Suppose a challenge offers eight visually plausible choices and
explicitly says:

> Choose whichever seems most appropriate.

There is no "correct" choice.

A human population might yield:

\[ P_H = \[0.12,0.16,0.09,0.14,0.11,0.15,0.13,0.10\] \]

while a model family might yield:

\[ P_A = \[0.03,0.05,0.04,0.61,0.08,0.07,0.06,0.06\] \]

Choice 4 is not "an AI answer." A human can choose it. But observing it
changes the likelihood ratio.

A single observation should rarely be decisive.

## 2.3 Sequential evidence

For challenge (C_i), response (R_i), and history (h_i):

\[ `\ell`{=tex}\_i = `\log`{=tex} `\frac{P(R_i\mid A,C_i,h_i)}`{=tex}
{P(R_i`\mid `{=tex}H,C_i,h_i)} \]

Accumulated evidence:

\[ L_n=`\sum`{=tex}\_{i=1}\^{n}w_i`\ell`{=tex}\_i \]

where (w_i) can down-weight correlated or weak evidence.

A calibrated posterior can then be estimated rather than treating (L_n)
directly as probability.

## 2.4 The critical insight

JANUS does **not** test whether an answer is random.

It tests whether the **conditional pattern of decisions** is
statistically more compatible with a human or artificial process.

Naive randomness tests would be easy to defeat. Distributional inference
is the core.

# 3. Cognitive divergence

Candidate divergence dimensions include:

-   semantic salience;
-   geometric salience;
-   symmetry preference;
-   compositional balance;
-   ambiguity resolution;
-   latent pattern detection;
-   preference under under-specification;
-   categorical boundary placement;
-   confidence calibration;
-   ordering preference;
-   sensitivity to irrelevant structure;
-   consistency across paraphrases;
-   sensitivity to adversarial distractors;
-   contextual anchoring;
-   temporal adaptation;
-   exploration versus exploitation;
-   response entropy;
-   conditional response entropy;
-   correction behavior;
-   cross-modal consistency.

No dimension should be assumed permanent. Every divergence is an
empirical hypothesis requiring continuous measurement.

# 4. Challenge families

## 4.1 Free-choice challenges

The subject chooses among several valid alternatives.

Example:

> Select whichever symbol feels most distinct.

The verifier knows population distributions, not a canonical answer.

## 4.2 Forced-choice ambiguity

Two or more interpretations are deliberately defensible.

The useful signal is how different populations resolve ambiguity.

## 4.3 Latent-structure challenges

Stimuli contain subtle mathematical, visual or semantic regularities.
Artificial systems may disproportionately detect or privilege the latent
feature.

The challenge should not require humans to detect the feature.

## 4.4 Semantic association

Subjects select, order or group concepts with deliberately loose
semantic relationships.

The system measures distributions, not semantic correctness.

## 4.5 Procedural visual challenges

Stimuli are generated at runtime from a grammar rather than selected
from a static bank.

Parameters may include:

-   topology;
-   orientation;
-   occlusion;
-   semantic category;
-   spatial relationships;
-   texture;
-   abstraction;
-   distractor family;
-   information density.

## 4.6 Conditional challenge chains

Challenge (C\_{i+1}) depends on prior interaction:

\[ C\_{i+1}=G(C_i,R_i,h_i,K_s) \]

where (K_s) is server-side secret state.

This prevents the session from being reducible to independent screenshot
classification.

## 4.7 Cross-modal challenges

A sequence can combine visual, linguistic and spatial decisions. This
should be used cautiously because accessibility must remain first-class.

# 5. Challenge generation

## 5.1 Generator requirements

A production generator should provide:

-   high instance diversity;
-   reproducibility from a server seed;
-   no dependency on a public answer bank;
-   measurable difficulty;
-   measurable divergence;
-   accessibility metadata;
-   versioned generator grammar;
-   deterministic reconstruction for audits;
-   cryptographically strong server-side seed generation.

## 5.2 Challenge descriptor

A conceptual descriptor:

``` json
{
  "challenge_id": "opaque-id",
  "family": "ambiguous_choice",
  "generator_version": "g3.4",
  "public_payload": {},
  "private_features": {},
  "calibration_version": "cal-2026-09",
  "expiry": 0
}
```

`private_features` must never be delivered to the client.

## 5.3 Server-secret selection

The discriminative property should not be trivially inferable from the
client payload or JavaScript bundle.

Challenge selection, scoring parameters and population calibration
belong server-side.

# 6. Adaptive selection

JANUS should select challenges by expected information gain rather than
arbitrary difficulty.

Let (Y`\in`{=tex}{H,A}) be the latent class.

A candidate challenge (C) can be scored by expected information gain:

\[
EIG(C)=H(Y`\mid `{=tex}h)-`\mathbb{E}`{=tex}\_{R}\[H(Y`\mid `{=tex}h,C,R)\]
\]

The selector seeks a challenge that is expected to reduce uncertainty
while satisfying constraints:

\[ C\^\*=`\arg`{=tex}`\max`{=tex}\_C EIG(C) \]

subject to:

-   accessibility;
-   latency budget;
-   fatigue budget;
-   challenge-family diversity;
-   correlation limits;
-   security policy;
-   fairness constraints.

This means two users need not receive the same sequence.

# 7. Telemetry

Telemetry is useful but dangerous. JANUS should explicitly separate
**cognitive evidence** from **auxiliary interaction evidence**.

The product should remain capable of evaluating the core research
hypothesis without covertly becoming a fingerprinting product.

## 7.1 Cognitive telemetry

Examples:

-   selected option;
-   option ordering;
-   sequence of selections;
-   response latency;
-   revision count;
-   confidence response, if explicitly requested;
-   challenge family;
-   semantic relation selected;
-   conditional transitions.

## 7.2 Interaction telemetry

Potential signals:

-   pointer path geometry;
-   pointer velocity;
-   acceleration;
-   click/tap timing;
-   hover duration;
-   touch trajectory;
-   scroll behavior;
-   focus/blur transitions;
-   keyboard navigation;
-   correction events;
-   abandonment/re-entry.

These are supporting signals, not definitions of humanity.

## 7.3 Environmental telemetry

Use sparingly:

-   coarse device class;
-   input modality;
-   viewport class;
-   rendering capability required by challenge;
-   coarse latency characteristics.

Avoid turning JANUS into invasive fingerprinting.

## 7.4 Explicitly discouraged by default

Unless separately justified:

-   cross-site identifiers;
-   persistent hardware fingerprinting;
-   unrelated browsing history;
-   invasive sensor collection;
-   raw biometrics;
-   unnecessary IP retention;
-   collection unrelated to verification.

## 7.5 Telemetry minimization

Every signal should answer:

1.  What hypothesis does it test?
2.  How much information gain does it provide?
3.  Can it be computed client-side and discarded?
4.  Can it be quantized?
5.  How long must it exist?
6.  Does it introduce demographic/accessibility bias?
7.  Can an attacker manipulate it trivially?

If there is no defensible answer, do not collect it.

# 9. Scoring engine

## 9.1 Bayesian framing

Given observations (O):

\[ P(H`\mid `{=tex}O)= `\frac{P(O\mid H)P(H)}`{=tex}
{P(O`\mid `{=tex}H)P(H)+P(O`\mid `{=tex}A)P(A)} \]

In reality, "AI" is heterogeneous. A better model is:

\[ A={A_1,A_2,`\ldots`{=tex},A_m,U} \]

where (A_i) represents known model/agent families and (U) represents
unknown/artificial out-of-distribution behavior.

## 9.2 Mixture model

\[ P(O`\mid `{=tex}A)=`\sum`{=tex}\_j `\pi`{=tex}\_j
P(O`\mid `{=tex}A_j) \]

This prevents JANUS from equating "AI" with one vendor or architecture.

## 9.3 Sequential Probability Ratio Test

A natural stopping strategy resembles SPRT.

Define thresholds (T_H) and (T_A).

-   if (L_n\<T_H): sufficient evidence for human-compatible interaction;
-   if (L_n\>T_A): sufficient evidence for artificial-compatible
    interaction;
-   otherwise: continue.

The thresholds should be calibrated to explicit false-positive and
false-negative targets.

## 9.4 Three-way outcome

Never force every session into binary certainty.

Recommended outcomes:

-   **PASS** --- sufficient evidence of human-compatible behavior;
-   **CHALLENGE/CONTINUE** --- insufficient evidence;
-   **REJECT/ESCALATE** --- strong evidence of automation under policy.

An **UNKNOWN** internal state is essential.

# 10. Calibration

JANUS lives or dies by calibration.

## 10.1 Human baseline

Collect opt-in research data from diverse humans across:

-   ages;
-   languages;
-   cultures;
-   devices;
-   accessibility needs;
-   input modalities;
-   expertise levels;
-   neurocognitive variation where ethically and lawfully studied.

The goal is not to define a narrow "normal human."

## 10.2 Artificial baseline

Test multiple:

-   model families;
-   model sizes;
-   multimodal systems;
-   temperatures;
-   decoding strategies;
-   agent scaffolds;
-   system prompts;
-   tool-enabled agents;
-   deliberate human-simulation prompts.

## 10.3 Adversarial baseline

The important benchmark is not:

> Can a naive model pass?

It is:

> Can a model that knows JANUS's objective produce human-compatible
> distributions without knowing the server's private discriminative
> hypothesis?

# 11. Divergence metrics

Candidate metrics:

### KL divergence

\[ D\_{KL}(P_H`\parallel `{=tex}P_A)= `\sum`{=tex}\_x
P_H(x)`\log`{=tex}`\frac{P_H(x)}{P_A(x)}`{=tex} \]

Useful but asymmetric and problematic with zero-probability bins.

### Jensen--Shannon divergence

\[ JSD(P_H,P_A)= `\frac`{=tex}12D\_{KL}(P_H`\parallel `{=tex}M)+
`\frac`{=tex}12D\_{KL}(P_A`\parallel `{=tex}M) \]

with:

\[ M=`\frac12`{=tex}(P_H+P_A) \]

Bounded and symmetric, often easier operationally.

### Total variation distance

\[ TV(P_H,P_A)=`\frac12`{=tex}`\sum`{=tex}\_x\|P_H(x)-P_A(x)\| \]

### Mutual information

Measure how informative response (R) is about class (Y):

\[ I(Y;R`\mid `{=tex}C) \]

Challenge selection should ultimately optimize predictive value under
real-world adversarial conditions, not worship one divergence metric.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Correlation problem

Evidence cannot simply be summed as if every challenge were independent.

If five challenges measure the same latent preference, naive
accumulation produces false confidence.

Mitigations:

-   challenge-family diversity;
-   covariance estimation;
-   hierarchical models;
-   learned correlation penalties;
-   maximum contribution caps per feature family;
-   calibration on complete sessions rather than isolated answers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Human error is not failure

A core JANUS design principle:

> Individual "AI-like" decisions must not equal rejection.

Humans are variable. Models can behave human-like. Statistical inference
only makes sense across uncertainty.

The system must tolerate:

-   accidental clicks;
-   rereading;
-   fast experts;
-   slow users;
-   unusual preferences;
-   accessibility tools;
-   atypical motor behavior;
-   different cultural associations.

The verifier scores evidence, not conformity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Accessibility

A CAPTCHA that excludes humans defeats its own purpose.

JANUS should maintain multiple equivalent challenge channels:

-   visual;
-   textual;
-   keyboard-first;
-   screen-reader compatible;
-   reduced-motion;
-   high-contrast;
-   motor-accessible.

The selector must compare each user against an appropriate calibrated
population for the interaction modality, not punish accessibility
technology for changing telemetry.

Accessibility mode must never itself become a negative signal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Privacy architecture

## 15.1 Principles

1.  Data minimization.
2.  Ephemeral identifiers.
3.  Purpose limitation.
4.  Short retention by default.
5.  Aggregation for long-term calibration.
6.  Separation of verification data from advertising/analytics.
7.  No cross-site tracking as a hidden dependency.
8.  Auditable feature registry.

## 15.2 Two data planes

**Verification plane:** ephemeral session-level evidence required to
make the current decision.

**Research plane:** de-identified, aggregated or consented samples used
to recalibrate distributions.

Do not silently merge them.

## 15.3 Feature registry

# 16. Threat model

## 16.1 Screenshot-to-model relay

Attacker forwards each challenge to a multimodal model.

Mitigation: conditional sessions, procedural generation, time-efficient
multi-step interaction, server-private challenge selection.

Do not rely on latency alone.

## 16.2 Human-response imitation

Attacker instructs:

> Answer like a random human.

This is the central JANUS threat.

Mitigation is not "detect randomness." Instead:

-   use conditional distributions;
-   select challenges with empirically measured adversarial separation;
-   rotate challenge families;
-   keep scoring hypotheses private;
-   benchmark explicit imitation prompts.

## 16.3 Learned human distribution

An attacker gathers human samples and trains a simulator of (P_H).

This is a serious theoretical threat.

JANUS responds through:

-   challenge novelty;
-   conditional adaptation;
-   private generator parameters;
-   continuous recalibration;
-   challenge retirement;
-   mixture modeling;
-   limiting public leakage of per-challenge scoring.

It cannot make this attack impossible in principle.

## 16.4 Replay

Mitigate with:

-   single-use challenge IDs;
-   expiry;
-   session binding;
-   nonce;
-   server verification;
-   signed challenge state.

## 16.5 Client tampering

Never trust client-side scores.

The browser can render and collect events, but final inference should be
server-authoritative.

## 16.6 Model extraction of JANUS

Attackers may query repeatedly to infer the decision boundary.

Mitigations:

-   rate limits;
-   coarse outcomes;
-   no raw risk score exposure;
-   randomized challenge selection;
-   monitoring systematic probing;
-   challenge retirement.

# 19. Session protocol

## Step 1 --- Initialize

Integrator requests a verification session.

Server creates:

-   session ID;
-   nonce;
-   policy profile;
-   expiry;
-   modality constraints;
-   prior risk assumptions.

## Step 2 --- Select

Selector chooses the challenge maximizing expected information under
usability constraints.

## Step 3 --- Generate

Generator creates a fresh instance from server-side entropy and
versioned grammar.

## Step 4 --- Present

Client renders only necessary public challenge state.

## Step 5 --- Observe

Client records minimal approved interaction features.

## Step 6 --- Submit

Response + signed/session-bound metadata are sent to server.

## Step 7 --- Infer

Scorer updates likelihoods/posterior.

## Step 8 --- Stop or continue

If confidence threshold is reached, finish. Otherwise select the next
challenge conditioned on history.

## Step 9 --- Attest

Server returns an opaque, short-lived verification token to the
integrator.

# 24. Do not build a mouse-jiggling detector

A major anti-pattern is treating motor telemetry as the essence of
humanity.

Bots can synthesize Bézier curves. Humans can use assistive technology.
Remote desktops alter events. Touchscreens differ from mice.

Motor telemetry can contribute weak evidence, but JANUS's differentiator
should remain **cognitive distributional divergence**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. The anti-overfitting rule

If a feature separates the current benchmark models but has no plausible
stability under model changes, treat it as fragile.

Research should report:

-   in-family performance;
-   out-of-family performance;
-   unseen-model performance;
-   adversarial-prompt performance;
-   temporal holdout performance.

The most valuable challenge is not necessarily the one with the highest
current separation. It is the one whose separation generalizes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 26. Unknown models and OOD behavior

A binary human-vs-known-AI classifier can mistakenly label novel AI
behavior as human.

JANUS therefore needs out-of-distribution detection.

Conceptually:

\[ P_H(O),`\quad `{=tex}P\_{A_1}(O),...,P\_{A_m}(O) \]

If all are low:

\[ `\max`{=tex}*k P_k(O)\<`\tau`{=tex}*{OOD} \]

the result should become **UNKNOWN**, not automatically HUMAN.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. Human distribution is multimodal

There is no single (P_H).

Better:

\[ P_H(O)=`\sum`{=tex}\_k `\omega`{=tex}*k P*{H_k}(O) \]

where subpopulations may correspond to interaction modality,
language/context or other ethically defensible calibration strata.

Do not infer sensitive attributes merely to improve CAPTCHA performance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28. Adversarial JANUS benchmark

Every release should test at least:

1.  naive model;
2.  model told it is a CAPTCHA;
3.  model told JANUS detects AI-like choices;
4.  model instructed to imitate humans;
5.  model given example human distributions;
6.  model with memory across challenges;
7.  multimodal agent controlling a browser;
8.  agent with deliberate randomization;
9.  ensemble of agents;
10. human-in-the-loop hybrid.

The meaningful metric is performance against the strongest realistic
attacker.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 29. Deliberate randomization attack

A model could simply choose uniformly.

That defeats only badly designed JANUS challenges.

If:

\[ P_A\^`\prime`{=tex}(R`\mid `{=tex}C)=U(R) \]

JANUS can include challenges where human distributions are themselves
strongly non-uniform, or where useful evidence exists in conditional
transitions rather than marginal choices.

The attacker then needs to approximate:

\[ P_H(R_i`\mid `{=tex}C_i,R\_{\<i},C\_{\<i}) \]

not merely add noise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 30. Human-simulator attack

The theoretical ideal attacker learns a generative human simulator
(S_H):

\[ R`\sim `{=tex}S_H(C,h) \]

If (S_H) becomes indistinguishable from actual human behavior over all
observable challenge dimensions, JANUS cannot reliably separate them.

This is not an implementation failure. It is an information-theoretic
boundary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 31. The JANUS limit

Suppose for every observable interaction sequence (X):

\[ P(X`\mid `{=tex}H)=P(X`\mid `{=tex}A) \]

Then:

\[ TV(P_H,P_A)=0 \]

and no classifier operating solely on (X) can outperform chance under
equal priors.

This is the **JANUS Limit**.

At that point, CAPTCHA in the original cognitive sense ceases to be
meaningful.

Anti-abuse can continue through:

-   identity;
-   authentication;
-   hardware attestation;
-   economic cost;
-   rate limits;
-   provenance;
-   account reputation.

But those are no longer Turing-style tests.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 32. JANUS as a benchmark for AI convergence

The irony is structural: every divergence JANUS publishes can become a
target for model developers.

Thus JANUS can double as a benchmark:

\[ JCS = 1-D(P_H,P_A) \]

where **JCS** = JANUS Cognitive Similarity.

A model approaching (JCS=1) increasingly reproduces calibrated human
response distributions.

JANUS therefore has two possible legacies:

1.  durable human verification;
2.  a benchmark that helps demonstrate when human/AI behavioral
    distinction has collapsed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 33. Success criteria

JANUS v1 should not claim to "solve CAPTCHA."

A credible v1 demonstrates:

-   at least several challenge families;
-   statistically significant human/model divergence;
-   replication across unseen model families;
-   adversarial imitation testing;
-   acceptable human completion time;
-   controlled false-positive rate;
-   accessibility path;
-   privacy-minimized telemetry;
-   measurable adaptive-selection benefit.

# 41. Fairness

A verifier can accidentally learn differences unrelated to human-vs-AI
status.

Examples:

-   native vs non-native language;
-   mouse vs touch;
-   age-related motor differences;
-   cultural semantic preferences.

Therefore evaluate:

\[ FPR_g=P(`\text{reject}`{=tex}`\mid `{=tex}H,g) \]

for relevant, ethically collected groups/modalities.

The objective is not merely low average FPR. It is avoiding unacceptable
subgroup disparities.

-   no tiny targets;

# 50. Statistical validation

For every claimed divergence:

-   define null hypothesis;
-   predefine metric where possible;

-   confidence intervals;
-   multiple-comparison correction;
-   holdout challenge instances;
-   holdout humans;
-   holdout models;
-   adversarial holdout.

Countermeasures:

-   procedural generation;
-   holdout grammars;
-   secret evaluation seeds;
-   temporal evaluation;
-   family-level generalization tests.

The objective is not to hide every stimulus forever. It is to make

# 68. Experimental example

Suppose challenge family `AMB-17` has four choices.

``` text

B  0.21
C  0.29
```


\[ LR=`\frac{0.72}{0.29}`{=tex}`\approx2.48`{=tex} \]

# 70. Entropy profile

For a challenge:

\[ H_H(R`\mid `{=tex}C)=-`\sum`{=tex}\_r
P_H(r`\mid `{=tex}C)`\log `{=tex}P_H(r`\mid `{=tex}C) \]

\[ H_A(R`\mid `{=tex}C)=-`\sum`{=tex}\_r
P_A(r`\mid `{=tex}C)`\log `{=tex}P_A(r`\mid `{=tex}C) \]

Sometimes models may be more concentrated; sometimes humans may be.
Neither direction should be assumed universally.

The discriminative feature is the **difference in distributions**, not
"AI is deterministic."

# 84. The philosophical endpoint

Classical CAPTCHA assumes a persistent hierarchy:

``` text
human capability > machine capability
```

JANUS assumes only a measurable difference:

``` text
human cognition ≠ artificial cognition
```

Its operation is possible while:

\[ D(P_H,P_A)\>0 \]

Its fundamental endpoint occurs when:

\[ D(P_H,P_A)`\rightarrow0`{=tex} \]

At that point, no increasingly bizarre puzzle restores the original
premise. A verifier must move from cognition to provenance, identity or
authorization.

JANUS should be designed to recognize that endpoint rather than disguise
it.

# 86. Genesis statement

JANUS began from a simple observation:

> Modern CAPTCHAs increasingly stop being CAPTCHAs because the machine
> can perform the same cognitive tasks as the human.

The response is not to make the picture blurrier, the rotation stranger
or the puzzle more irritating.

The response is to change the question.

Traditional CAPTCHA asks:

> **Can you do what a machine cannot?**

JANUS asks:

> **Given freedom to respond, what kind of cognition generated your
> choices?**

The first paradigm measures capability.

JANUS measures divergence.

And if one day there is no divergence left to measure, JANUS has
discovered the boundary at which CAPTCHA itself no longer has meaning.

# Appendix A --- Minimal mathematical model

Let:

\[ Y`\in`{=tex}{H,A} \]

For challenge sequence (C\_{1:n}), responses (R\_{1:n}), telemetry
(T\_{1:n}):

\[ O_i=(C_i,R_i,T_i) \]

Posterior odds:

\[ `\frac{P(A\mid O_{1:n})}{P(H\mid O_{1:n})}`{=tex} =
`\frac{P(A)}{P(H)}`{=tex} `\prod`{=tex}\_{i=1}\^{n} `\frac{
P(R_i,T_i\mid A,C_i,h_i)
}{
P(R_i,T_i\mid H,C_i,h_i)
}`{=tex} \]

In practice dependencies require richer joint models and calibrated
likelihoods.

Challenge selection:

\[ C\_{i+1} = `\arg`{=tex}`\max`{=tex}\_C `\mathbb{E}`{=tex} `\left[

# Appendix B --- Minimal pseudocode

``` text
function verify(session):
    posterior = session.prior

    while true:
        if posterior.human >= PASS_THRESHOLD:
            return PASS

        if posterior.artificial >= ESCALATE_THRESHOLD:
            return ESCALATE

        if session.challenge_count >= MAX_CHALLENGES:
            return UNKNOWN

        challenge = selector.choose(
            posterior=posterior,
            history=session.history,
            constraints=session.constraints
        )

        observation = present_and_collect(challenge)

        features = feature_engine.extract(observation)

        likelihoods = calibration.likelihood(
            challenge=challenge,
            features=features,
            history=session.history
        )

        posterior = scorer.update(
            posterior,
            likelihoods
        )

        session.append(challenge, observation, features)
```

# Appendix D --- Non-goals

JANUS does not claim to:

-   detect consciousness;
-   prove biological humanity;
-   replace authentication;
-   eliminate all bots;
-   prevent human CAPTCHA farms;
-   provide permanent separation from future AI;
-   justify invasive tracking;
-   classify a person from one unusual choice.

It is a probabilistic verification framework built around measurable
human--AI response divergence.

# Appendix E --- One-sentence specification

> **JANUS is an adaptive, sequential CAPTCHA architecture that generates
> low-friction challenges with no necessary correct answer, measures
> conditional divergence between calibrated human and artificial-agent
> response distributions, incorporates privacy-minimized telemetry as
> auxiliary evidence, and stops when sufficient evidence---or
> irreducible uncertainty---has been reached.**
